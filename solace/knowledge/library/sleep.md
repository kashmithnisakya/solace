## Steady routine
Going to bed and getting up at roughly the same time every day, including weekends, helps the body learn when to sleep. A wind-down time before bed, such as a warm shower, reading, or gentle stretching, tells the body it is nearly time to rest.

## When you can't fall asleep
If you have been lying awake for a long time, getting up and doing something calm in dim light until you feel sleepy is often better than lying there frustrated. Trying hard to sleep usually backfires.

## Things that get in the way
Caffeine later in the day, heavy meals or alcohol close to bedtime, and bright screens in bed can all make sleep harder. A dark, quiet, cool room helps.

## A racing mind at night
Writing worries or a to-do list on paper before bed can help the mind let go of them until morning. Slow breathing or relaxing each muscle group in turn can also calm the body.

## When to get help
If poor sleep goes on for weeks and affects daily life, a doctor can help. Long-lasting sleep problems are common with stress, anxiety and low mood, and they are treatable.
