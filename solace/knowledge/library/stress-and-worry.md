## Grounding when stress hooks you
Stress often shows up as "getting hooked" by difficult thoughts and feelings, so that a person stops noticing the world around them. A simple way back is to slow down and notice: press your feet into the floor, slowly stretch, take a slow breath, then look around and name a few things you can see and hear. Then gently bring attention back to what you are doing right now. It does not make the feelings disappear, it gives you a little room around them.

## Unhooking from difficult thoughts
Instead of arguing with a painful thought, you can notice it and name it: "I'm having the thought that I'll fail", or "Here's worry again." Naming a thought puts a little distance between you and it. Thoughts are words and pictures in the mind, not orders you must follow.

## Acting on what matters
Even under stress, small actions in line with your values help: being kind to someone, helping a friend, doing one useful task. Ask yourself what kind of person you want to be in this hard time, and pick one tiny thing that fits.

## Making room for feelings
Painful feelings are a normal reaction to hard situations. Pushing them away often makes them stronger. You can notice where the feeling is in your body, breathe into it, and let it be there for a moment, like weather passing through.
