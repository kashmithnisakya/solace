## Loving-kindness (metta)
The Metta Sutta describes wishing all beings to be happy and safe, with a mind as boundless as a mother's love for her only child. Metta practice can begin with oneself: quietly repeating wishes like "May I be safe, may I be well, may I be at ease." It can soften harsh self-criticism.

## Impermanence
A core Buddhist teaching is that all conditions change. Painful feelings and hard times, too, arise and pass. This is not a reason to dismiss pain, but it can give hope that this moment is not forever.

## Mind and anger
The Dhammapada opens by teaching that the mind shapes our experience, and later says that hatred is never ended by hatred, only by non-hatred. Gently noticing thoughts, without blaming oneself for them, is part of the path.

## Faith and seeking help
Caring for the mind with a doctor or counsellor fits well with Buddhist practice. Monks and teachers have long encouraged people to seek care for illness of body and mind.
